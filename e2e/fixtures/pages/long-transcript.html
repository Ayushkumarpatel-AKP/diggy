Talk transcript — “Shipping Rust 1.85 in production”

[00:00] Welcome, everyone. Today I want to walk through what Rust 1.85 changed for us and how we rolled it out to a service that serves about two hundred thousand requests a minute. Nothing here is exotic; it is a practical migration story with the sharp edges left in.

[01:10] First, the headline feature: async closures are stable in Rust 1.85. For years we wrote closures that returned futures by hand, boxing them and paying an allocation on every call. Async closures let the compiler infer the future type, so a closure that captures a reference and returns a future now compiles without the boxing dance. The ergonomics win is real, and the codegen is honestly better than our hand-rolled version.

[03:40] Second, the Rust 2024 edition. We enabled it crate by crate. The migration was mostly mechanical — the edition guide handled the bulk — but two things bit us. The new `gen` keyword collided with a module name, and one unsafe block needed the new `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` reasoning. Both were five-minute fixes once the compiler pointed at them.

[06:25] Third, toolchain hygiene. We pinned the minimum supported Rust version to 1.82 for the build, even though 1.85 is what we compile with, so downstream consumers are not forced forward. The `rustup update stable` path is what most people will run, and it just works.

[08:05] Then we get to the migration itself. We ran the new toolchain in CI on a branch for two weeks before cutting over. The only regression was a timing-sensitive test that we had been getting away with; the new async closures changed the scheduling enough to expose it. We fixed the test, not the compiler.

[11:30] Deployment: we shipped the new binary behind a flag, watched error rates for an hour, then flipped it cluster by cluster. Rollback was a single flag. If you take one thing from this talk, make it that: ship behind a flag you can flip.

[14:50] Questions. “Do async closures help with trait bounds?” Sometimes — the inference is better, but you still hit the usual higher-ranked lifetime limits. “Should I migrate now?” If you are on 1.82 or newer, yes; the edition migration is a weekend, not a quarter.

[17:20] Thanks for coming. Slides and the migration checklist are in the repo linked on the conference site. Go upgrade, then go take the afternoon off.
